Abstract—Surface water quality monitoring is essential for public health, biodiversity, and municipal water security. Traditional laboratory assays, such as 5-day Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD5), suffer from a 120-hour incubation latency. This paper presents Phase 1 of an applied machine learning investigation predicting the Canadian Council of Ministers of the Environment Water Quality Index (CCME WQI) across 2,827,977 multi-national sensor observations spanning 83 years (1940–2023) across 5 nations (England, USA, Ireland, China, and Canada). A data audit disproved legacy claims of missingness, confirming 100% complete-case ground truth. Thermodynamic boundary screening purged 8,360 corrupted telemetry entries (0.296%), yielding 2,819,617 clean observations. We engineered 38 domain features incorporating non-linear log(1+x) transformations, aquatic stoichiometry (Redfield N:P ratio, Benson-Krause DO saturation, Emerson un-ionized ammonia), and chronological station lags. Benchmarked across a stratified 70/15/15 partition, Random Forest (R² = 0.9999, MAE = 0.052) and XGBoost (R² = 0.9996, MAE = 0.137) dramatically outperformed the mean baseline (MAE = 14.130). Game-theoretic TreeSHAP feature attribution identified orthophosphate as the dominant predictive driver (accounting for >88% of split decisions). We formalize a production modular package (src/) validated by a 12-test automated unit test suite (pytest) and provide an equitable six-member contribution matrix meeting Phase 1 CSAI evaluation requirements.

I. Introduction

Freshwater security is under severe anthropogenic pressure from agricultural nutrient runoff, sewage discharge, and industrial contamination. Standard monitoring workflows rely on discrete physical grab sampling and wet-lab analytical assays. However, assays such as BOD5 require multi-day microbiological incubation, preventing immediate detection of acute chemical spills.

Machine learning offers high-throughput surrogate inference, translating real-time probe telemetry directly into continuous indices. While tree ensembles are effective on tabular data, naive models suffer from: (i) sensor corruption artifacts, (ii) heavy nutrient skewness spanning four orders of magnitude, (iii) circular target leakage, and (iv) uninterpretable decision boundaries. Our Phase 1 pipeline explicitly overcomes each of these challenges.

II. Related Work & Gap Analysis

Classical models relied on differential equations like the Streeter-Phelps oxygen sag formula (1925), which require expensive bathymetric tuning and fail to scale across basins. Breiman (2001) introduced Random Forests, demonstrating variance reduction via bootstrap bagging. Chen & Guestrin (2016) developed XGBoost, using regularized second-order Taylor gradients for rapid tabular modeling. Lundberg & Lee (2017) formalized TreeSHAP for exact polynomial-time cooperative game-theoretic feature attribution. We resolve key literature gaps by enforcing thermodynamic screening, preventing target leakage, and capturing non-lookahead station memory.

III. Mathematical Formulation

A. CCME Water Quality Index Formulation

The CCME WQI synthesizes multi-parameter compliance into a continuous score bounded in [0, 100]:

CCME WQI = 100 − [ √(F₁² + F₂² + F₃²) / 1.732 ]

where 1.732 = √3 normalizes the maximum distance from ideal (0,0,0). F₁ (Scope) is the percentage of failed parameters; F₂ (Frequency) is the percentage of failed tests; and F₃ (Amplitude) scales excursion magnitude through the normalized sum of excursions (nse):

nse = (∑ excursionᵢ) / N_total,   F₃ = nse / (0.01·nse + 0.01)

B. Random Forest & XGBoost Ensembles

Random Forest aggregates B de-correlated trees trained on bootstrap samples: y_hat = (1/B) ∑ T_b(x). XGBoost minimizes a regularized objective via second-order Taylor approximation:

L_tilde ≈ ∑ [ gᵢ f_t(xᵢ) + 0.5 hᵢ f_t²(xᵢ) ] + γ T_L + 0.5 λ ∑ wⱼ²

yielding optimal leaf weights wⱼ* = − (∑ gᵢ) / (∑ hᵢ + λ).

V. Domain Feature Engineering

We constructed 38 features across 7 categories:

1. Benson-Krause DO Saturation: DO_sat(T) is computed via an empirical polynomial of temperature. We derive DO_%sat = (DO/DO_sat)*100 and DO_deficit = max(0, DO_sat − DO).

2. Redfield Stoichiometric Ratio: N:P = Nitrogen / (Orthophosphate + 1e-5), identifying whether phosphorus or nitrogen limits algal growth.

3. Emerson Toxic Ammonia: Quantifies lethal un-ionized NH3 fraction as a function of temperature and pH.

4. log(1+x) Normalization: Applied y = ln(1+x) to skewed nutrients. Linear correlation between orthophosphate and WQI surged from r = −0.66 to r = −0.85.

5. Station Memory: Chronological shift-1 station lags (hist_ortho_lag1) achieved rank correlation ρ = −0.78 with WQI with zero forward lookahead leakage.

VII. Interpretability via TreeSHAP

Game-theoretic TreeSHAP feature attribution confirmed that orthophosphate drives over 88% of all decision tree splits. Because phosphorus is freshwater's critical limiting nutrient, elevated concentrations trigger immediate WQI collapse. Shift-1 station lag (hist_ortho_lag1) ranked second, proving the power of local environmental memory.

VIII. Code Quality & Automated Testing

We engineered a modular package (src/) with features, models, metrics, and utils. A 12-test automated unit test suite executed under pytest passes in 1.65 seconds (100% pass rate), verifying thermodynamic boundaries, stoichiometry, anti-leakage quarantine, and model serialization.
